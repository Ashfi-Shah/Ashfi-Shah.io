Architecture

Read more

I believe that architecture always tells a story. Some buildings offer the comfort and convenience of modern design, while others carry decades or even centuries of history, with stories waiting to be discovered. I appreciate the benefits of modernity while remaining fascinated by the stories that previous generations have left behind through their buildings.

Beyond appreciating architecture, I enjoy analysing how existing facilities can be improved and imagining new spaces that combine functionality, comfort and thoughtful design.

While I am neither an architect nor an engineer, I enjoy exploring architecture as a hobbyist, guided by curiosity, creativity and an appreciation for the built environment.

Urban Design

Read more

Urbanism is an important part of my personality. I enjoy exploring how different cities develop their own identities, respond to changing needs and continuously improve. I believe that urban design is an essential expression of civic identity, reflecting the history, values and aspirations of a community.

I am particularly fascinated by how cities have evolved over time, from the compact, walkable downtowns of earlier centuries to the automobile-centred planning of the mid-20th century and the growing emphasis on sustainability, public transportation and active mobility in the 21st century.

Some cities whose urban design I particularly admire include:

  • Toronto, Ontario: A dynamic city with robust public transportation, dedicated cycling infrastructure and a fascinating mixture of historic and modern architecture. Its continuous development of new neighbourhoods and infrastructure makes it especially interesting to follow.
  • Thunder Bay, Ontario: A relatively stable city that continues to pursue new initiatives and improvements. I appreciate its cycling infrastructure and revitalisation projects, particularly the transformation of the Victoriaville area.
  • Quebec City, Quebec: A city rich in history that makes a remarkable effort to preserve its architectural and cultural heritage. Exploring its historic neighbourhoods offers an immersive experience and a strong connection to the past.
  • Kingston, Ontario: A historic city that successfully combines heritage with modern amenities, demonstrating that preservation and contemporary development can coexist.
  • Amsterdam, Netherlands: An inspiring example of sustainable urbanism, with a strong emphasis on public transportation, cycling infrastructure and reducing dependence on private automobiles.

For me, urbanism is about understanding what makes a city distinctive and imagining how thoughtful planning can make it an even better place to live.

History

Read more

History following the beginning of the Industrial Revolution, around 1750, particularly fascinates me. In fewer than 300 years, humanity has undergone extraordinary social, political and technological transformations. We have developed democracies, found cures and treatments for numerous diseases, grown in our appreciation of one another, confronted shared challenges, invented remarkable technologies and even ventured into distant corners of the universe.

To me, history is the continuing story of humanity: a story that began long before us and will continue long after us. Understanding the past allows us to appreciate how far we have come, recognise the challenges we have faced and imagine what the future might hold.

My particular interests include Canadian history, American history, world history since 1914, the local histories of cities that hold a special place in my heart, and my own family history.

Hobbies

Cycling

Read more

One of my favourite activities is cycling, especially around Kingston. Cycling is a healthy and environmentally sustainable way to commute, and I am always looking for an opportunity to get out on my bicycle. I own a mountain bike, affectionately given the vessel-style name The Scarborough.

My favourite cycling route begins at the intersection of Princess and Division Streets. From there, I travel south along Division Street, west along Brock Street, south along Palace Road, and west along Johnson Street. I then head south through Queen's University's West Campus, east along Union Street, and north along either Division Street or University Avenue to return to Princess Street.

I am always eager to explore new routes. In 2027, I am planning a long-distance ride from Kingston to Belleville, Ontario!

Fun fact: I learned how to ride a bicycle at the age of 22! In fact, I had already driven on Ontario Highway 401 before learning how to ride a bike.
